# Using PlantCare

PlantCare helps you keep a personal collection, schedule care and record what
you have done. The current environment is a staging beta at
<https://staging.plantacare.org>, not a production release. Use synthetic test
data while the launch requirements remain open. The reviewed catalog dataset includes 210 houseplant, crop and tree entries,
including 68 named cultivars, with linked care references and licensed photos. Its preserved
legacy demo entry is synthetic and is not botanical guidance.

The catalog combines name search, light requirement and a Cats and dogs filter.
Reviewed as non-toxic includes only plants with a linked cat-and-dog classification
reference, rather than every plant without a warning. Open details to see the
reference. Non-toxic plants can still cause stomach upset if eaten. Clear filters
restores the full catalog.

Choose Growth references → With growth photos to find species with example
photographs. Their cards show Growth photos available; open View details for the
gallery and credits. This filter combines with the others and remains through
comparison, details and reload. Clear filters resets it while keeping comparison
selections. The photographs are reference examples, without a fixed growth timeline.

## Your first plant

1. Create an account and use the confirmation link sent to your email address.
   Check spam if it does not arrive; the sign-in flow also offers confirmation
   resend and password recovery. Keep email links private.
2. Sign in, browse the plant catalog and open a species. Add it to My plants.
3. Give the individual plant a nickname. Add a location, tags and notes if useful.
   Avoid sensitive personal information in these fields.
4. Review the initial care dates and schedules. Choose intervals or weekdays as
   appropriate, and check the saved time zone and displayed next-due time.
   Suggested intervals are starting points; actual needs depend on the plant
   and its conditions.
5. Find the plant in My plants using search, location/tag filters or sorting.

## Browse the catalog

Use Find a plant to search common/scientific names, reviewed alternative names or descriptions. Combine it
with Light requirement and Name A–Z / Name Z–A sorting. These are the listed care
requirements; open View details for placement advice and full care instructions.
Your search and filters remain when you return from details or reload. Clear
filters restores the full catalog in A–Z order. Suggested watering intervals are
prompts to check moisture, not instructions to water an already wet pot.

Select Compare on two or three catalog cards, then choose Compare selected plants.
The comparison shows their care guidance and sources together. Search and filters
keep your selection; Clear filters keeps it too. Use Remove or Clear comparison
to change it. The comparison URL contains public species choices and can be
bookmarked. An exact scientific-name search identifies that species; common names
can still match more than one plant.

English common names lead the species listings; scientific names are secondary
and remain searchable. Your own plants use the nickname you saved. Monstera,
spider plant, golden pothos, aloe vera, jade plant, moth orchid, peace lily and wax
plant details also include Growth references with credited example photos.
These show different specimens, not a fixed growth timeline or a health diagnosis.

The catalog has 262 alternative names across 131 entries. Try Devils ivy or
mother in laws tongue; matching ignores apostrophe and hyphen variations. Species
details list Also known as. A shared name such as Ribbon plant can return several
different species, so compare the photos and scientific names before choosing.
My plants search also recognizes these alternative names while keeping the
nickname you saved and the selected location/tag filters.

## Garden plans and harvest records

Use the catalog category tabs to browse fruit, vegetables, roots/tubers, herbs and
trees. Entry type distinguishes general plants from named cultivars. A labelled
species reference photo does not verify the appearance of the named cultivar.

Garden shows your active crops and trees grouped by the locations saved in My
plants. Search, category and planting-plan filters apply to both the cards and
the Planting calendar. On an individual plant's detail page, save optional target
dates for sowing, planting out and expected harvest. Browse these in Garden by
month; each nickname/action event opens its own plant. Targets do not send new
reminders or mark any work complete.

On a plant's detail page, use Harvest records to record what you actually picked:
choose the date, optionally enter a quantity and unit, and add notes if useful.
Items must be whole numbers; grams/kilograms allow up to three decimal places.
Choose Record harvest to save. A failed save retains the draft for retry.

Use Remove harvest, then Confirm removal, to correct a mistake by deleting the
record; Keep harvest cancels. The latest 100 records are displayed, newest harvest
date first; older records remain saved and are included in Download my data.
Different units are not summed. Harvests do not change target dates, care history,
schedules or shared-calendar events. Archived plants keep readable harvests;
restore the plant before adding or removing records.

In Garden, choose Harvest overview to see recorded harvests from active garden
plants. It starts with this year through today. Set Harvest from and Harvest to,
then Apply harvest dates; a range can span up to 366 days. This year resets it.
The Garden category, search and planting-plan filters also apply to these totals.
Items, grams and kilograms remain separate; records without quantities are counted.
Totals include every matching record, while Recent harvests shows only matching
rows from the latest 100 across the garden for those dates. Open a plant link
(nickname first) to inspect its harvest log. Archived plants are excluded here;
their individual records remain available on archived plant details.

Harvest trends compares every matching record by month and plant within the
applied dates. Empty months appear too; partial months include only selected
days. Quantities keep their original units, with separate counts for records
without quantities. Plant names use nicknames first and link to details. These
tables describe saved records, without predicting yields or comparing unlike
units. They are not restricted to the latest 100 recent rows.

## Daily care and calendar

Open Dashboard to review due care and record an action when performed. Verify
the resulting care history and next-due date. A notification is a reminder;
opening it or marking it read does not record the care action.

Calendar offers Month, Week and Agenda views and plant/action/status filters.
An event shows the plant nickname and care action. In your own calendar:

- The main event link opens that individual plant's detail page.
- Care details opens the event panel; eligible due care can be completed there.
- The personal detail page links to care history, Dashboard and species details.

Shared calendars are read-only. Their entries open the shared event panel and
do not grant access to the owner's private plant detail page.

Archiving a plant hides it from the active collection and stops new care
reminders. Use Show archived to find it and restore it. Archiving is not erasure.

## Reminders

On a current generated reminder in Dashboard or Notifications, choose 1 hour,
24 hours or 3 days under Remind me in. Wait for the saved snoozed-until time.
Snoozing does not record care or change its due date, so Dashboard/calendar may
still show the action as overdue. Complete care only when you have performed it.
Completing, disabling or archiving that care prevents the delayed reminder.
Opening or marking a reminder read is separate from snoozing. Previously delivered
phone notifications cannot reliably be recalled. Check the release record for
[snoozing deployment status](release/reminder-snoozing.md).

In Notifications, choose in-app and push delivery, care actions, and reminder
lead time, then choose Save preferences. To receive push on a particular device,
also choose Enable push reminders there and allow the browser permission.
Changing a global preference does not grant permission on a new device.

A newly received care push opens Dashboard. Browser permissions, device battery
settings, network access and the push provider can delay or prevent delivery.
Review Dashboard even if no push arrives. Disable on this device removes that
device's subscription; browser/site settings also control notification permission.

New care reminders use a cartoon-style icon for the action: a water drop, plant
food, misting spray, pruning shears or a pot with soil. These are static drawings;
the phone controls how they appear and its separate app/status-bar symbol.
The reminder still opens Dashboard. Already delivered notifications keep their
original appearance.

## Install on Android and update

Open the HTTPS staging site in Chrome. Use Chrome's menu to install the app;
depending on the browser version this may be Install app or Add to Home screen
followed by Install. Open the resulting PlantCare icon to use the standalone app.
If the option is missing, return online, reload, and check whether it is already
installed. See [support](../SUPPORT.md) if it remains unavailable.

When PlantCare offers Update now, save unfinished input and accept the update.
It reloads the app with the new version. A routine update does not require
reinstallation or clearing site storage. Those actions can remove local settings
and push permission/subscriptions, so do not use them as the first troubleshooting
step. Server-side data remains associated with your account.

If opened offline, the app shows an offline explanation. Offline care entry and
full offline plant access are not supported. Return online and reload/reopen;
sign in if requested, then check the current collection and care history.

## Calendar feeds and sharing

Calendar can generate a private read-only subscription URL. Treat the entire URL
like a password: anyone who has it can read the feed. Save it when generated;
PlantCare cannot show the original URL again. Regenerate or revoke it if exposed.
An external calendar may retain previously downloaded entries after revocation.
External calendar clients refresh on their own schedule.

You can also share your calendar by email and revoke that access later. Gmail
addresses work too. Existing accounts receive an email link to read-only access.
New recipients get a seven-day invitation: create an account with the invited
address, confirm it, then open the invitation link to accept. Use Send email on
an existing share or pending invitation to resend a missed message; resending a
pending invitation replaces its earlier link. Delivery failures are shown; check
spam as well. You can revoke pending invitations before they are accepted. Check the
intended recipient before sharing. This does not add events to Google Calendar. Real
calendar-client import/revocation acceptance remains a release check.
Google and Microsoft account synchronization are disabled in this staging setup
and are not part of the current launch commitment.

## Your account and data

The new Account page offers Download my data and Permanently delete my account.
Enter your current password for either action; deletion additionally requires
typing DELETE. Export first if you want a private copy. A successful deletion
signs you out and removes active account/plant/care records. Connected external
calendars must be disconnected first. Existing encrypted backups, delivered
messages and copies held by calendar clients are separate. Backup cleanup targets
30 days but can be delayed if storage is unavailable or verification fails.
A minimal deletion marker is kept to prevent restoration during managed recovery.
See [deployment/enabling status](release/retention-and-erasure-recovery.md).
