# Private privacy-request procedure

Prepared 2026-10-21. Operator: Nicolas Arevalo Hoelscher, Munich, Germany.
Private contact: nico.arevalo2325@gmail.com. This procedure authorizes no new
email messages, account deletions, provider deletions or retention-policy changes.

1. Receive requests privately. Redirect public issue reports without copying
   their private contents into another public comment. Do not commit requests,
   exports, message bodies or verification material to Git.
2. Record a minimal private reference, received date, request type, identity-check
   status, applicable response deadline and outcome. Keep records separate from
   the public repository. Determine a proportionate retention criterion before
   collecting extra evidence; avoid government-ID copies by default.
3. Prefer signed-in, password-verified Account export/deletion. If sign-in fails,
   offer ordinary recovery. Email-address knowledge alone is not proof of control.
   Ask only for proportionate additional verification where necessary; never ask
   for passwords, reset/confirmation links, feed URLs or push keys.
4. Review scope: account export is useful but is not the whole access-request
   response. Consider support correspondence, technical records, provider-held
   email data, sharing recipients and retained erasure markers. Protect other
   people's data and do not include infrastructure secrets in a response.
5. For correction, use supported account/plant controls where possible; investigate
   unsupported fields privately. For restrictions/objections or disputes about
   erasure markers, escalate to the operator and document the decision. Do not
   invent an automatic restriction feature or remove the recovery safeguard.
6. For verified erasure, explain active-data removal, delayed guarded backup
   expiry, minimal markers and external-copy limits. Provider connections must
   first be disconnected. Do not ask a person to post an account ID publicly.
   Review separately any applicable provider deletion, correspondence and raw
   recovery artifacts; no blanket filesystem/provider cleanup is authorized here.
7. Respond without undue delay, normally within one month of receipt. Review the
   applicable rules for complex requests/extensions and notify the requester
   within the initial period when an extension applies. Explain any refusal,
   complaint route and judicial remedy rather than silently closing the request.
8. Record completion with minimal metadata, review retained copies and protect
   access to the response. Account recovery/reconciliation must continue following
   [the managed recovery safeguards](retention-and-erasure-recovery.md).

Source: [European Commission request-handling guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/dealing-requests-individuals_en),
reviewed 2026-10-21. This written procedure has not been tested as a complete
human support workflow; existing automated Account tests cover only their stated
technical scope. An [agent-led synthetic tabletop review](privacy-request-tabletop.md)
has now walked through eight fictional scenarios. Owner-led mailbox/process
acceptance remains required before wider launch.
